Through the Eyes of an Engineer

Western medicine is not evil in the way people think.

It's something more subtle, and in many ways more dangerous:

a system that -behind curtains and protocol- unravels the very fabric of a human life, blind to the spiraling chain of damage it amplifies with every intervention.
I have spent my life watching the "care" system flatten, erode, and torque the people I love - my grandmother, my father, my mother, my brother, my girlfriend - I see the pattern clearly and now want to name it.
Most people think medical harm comes from accidents. But the truth is harsher:
Harm comes from inertia, from refusal to change,
from the system choosing itself over the patient. If that isn't malice, what word do we use?
Harm repeated, harm predictable, harm uncorrected - these are not accidents.
THE GRINDSTONE

My mother lived to 94, because she had a supporting scaffolding:

micronutrients, antioxidants, routines, mitochondrial support, hydration rhythms, calm sleep, gentle responsive self-care.
This was the architecture that held her body and her mind throughout her years. But the moment she entered the hospital system, that scaffolding disappeared. Hospitals don't remove disease -
they remove context, identity,

stability.

They grind a whole person down into a two-dimensional paper chart.

In the span of hours, they take away the things that have kept a person alive for decades.
Not from malice - from basic medical principles applied wrongly, reflexively, unthinkingly.
But thoughtlessness repeated is still a form of harm. When the grindstone turns, there is only one way it ends.
THE STEAMROLLER

The steamroller is the unstoppable force of protocol. It doesn't matter what your hydration status really is. It matters what the checkbox says.
It doesn't matter that your leg swelling has been stable for 25 years. It matters that the script says "edema ? diuretic."
It doesn't matter that your hernia suddenly reduced, the obstruction vanished, the symptoms reversed.
It matters that "surgery is scheduled for tomorrow."

I watched a surgeon try to lay out a plan for my 90-year-old mother, completely oblivious to the fact that the problem was gone.
He wasn't thinking anymore. He was following momentum.
A system that can't stop itself, when reality changes is not only dangerous - it's defective and immoral.
And if a system refuses to fix the defect, what else do we call it?
THE FLEX-LIFE TEST

The most destructive force in Western medicine is the one no one talks about:

the back-and-forth physiological whiplash that breaks older adults like bending steel until it snaps.
Low blood pressure ? pressor drugs ? high blood pressure ? diuretics ? low blood pressure again ? overhydration ? kidney stress ? electrolyte collapse ? arrhythmias
? ICU ? heart failure ? death.
I've seen this destructive flex-test cycle more times than I care to count. It's slow, procedural, insidious.
And that is what makes it so lethal. Because these aren't "mistakes."
These are predictable outcomes of a system that bends people back and forth up and down, until they break.
Accident?

Not after the tenth time. Not after the hundredth. Not after the thousandth.
At some point, "not intended" becomes indistinguishable from "not cared about." And people die from the difference.
THE ORIGIN STORY

I was a freshman engineering student when I first saw the medical mask slip. My grandmother, newly started on digitalis, suddenly couldn't eat.
Nauseated at the sight of food.

The cardiologist stroked his beard, and proclaimed, "She has a death wish." I said, "Could it be digitalis overdose?"
His response:

"I don't like you. You should leave the room." Not because I was wrong.
Because I was right.

He had invoked the doctors' "catch-all" diagnosis while ignoring her body.

I had done some quick reading - Medicine 101 - and I knew what he did not, or simply forgot.
The blood work came back in red:

DIGITALIS TOXICITY.

He stopped the drug.

The next day she ate a hamburger the size of her fist. That's when I realized:
the problem wasn't just individual doctors.

It was the entire architecture of unquestionable authority and unexamined assumptions.
Patients (and their advocates) know their systems better than the system knows their patients.
That line has stayed true all my life.

WE ARE LOSING PEOPLE TO INSTITUTIONAL INDIFFERENCE

Not to malpractice, and not to villains,
but to something much harder to fight: Indifference
Arrogance Inertia Fragile egos

Closed protocol loops Reflex medicine
The worship of titles

The erasure of individuality

The refusal to redesign a broken machine

When a system repeatedly does harm and refuses to evolve, it isn't an accident anymore.
It becomes something darker:

a machine that preserves itself, even when it destroys loved ones. That's what I've seen.
Again and again.

And I know I'm not alone.

THE CALL

If you've ever watched a loved one slowly worn down by the grindstone, flattened under the steamroller,
or snapped by the flex-life cycle, then you already know this truth:
We cannot keep pretending this is acceptable. People should not be passengers in their own care. They should be their own primary physicians,
with doctors as consultants, not gods.
Because the system will not change unless people demand it. And the demand begins not with anger -

but with recognition.

The kind that makes your temperature rise. The kind that makes you stand up straighter. The kind that says:
"Never again. Not on my watch." That's the beginning of grassroots. That's the beginning of reform.
That's the beginning of human medicine. Not Western medicine.
Not protocol medicine. Not checkbox medicine. Human. Medicine.
And that -

that is worth fighting for.
